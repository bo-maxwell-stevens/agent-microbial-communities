## 4) Knowledge gaps
- Few studies jointly synthesize cross-domain coupling with both environmental and plant-diversity explanatory layers in one integrated framework.
- AMF-centered pair interpretation is often under-developed relative to BAC-inclusive networks.
- Dark-diversity/completeness frameworks are rarely connected to microbial coupling interpretation.
- Many network studies do not fully reconcile compositional constraints with biological interpretation.

## 5) Why this study is needed
- Existing outputs already establish coupling hierarchy, environmental driver structure, and plant-diversity modulation; integrating these layers addresses the above gap without introducing post-hoc exploratory analyses.

## 6) Explicit hypotheses supported by completed analyses
- H1: Coupling hierarchy differs by pair (BAC↔ITS and EUK↔ITS strongest).
- H2: Environmental filtering contributes pair-specific explanatory variation, with recurring pH-linked signal.
- H3: Plant-diversity terms provide incremental explanatory gain beyond abiotic baselines.
- H4: AMF-centered pairings are weaker in coupling strength but retain plant-associated contributions.
- H5: Integrated synthesis can classify pair/branch combinations by coupling + environment + plant support.

## 7) Candidate discussion themes
- Cross-domain modularity vs shared structuring gradients.
- pH as a persistent integrative axis across microbial domains.
- Plant diversity as context-dependent modulator (not universal driver).
- AMF-centered ecological interpretation and limitations.
- Methodological caution: compositional data and network inference uncertainty.
