# Results Storyboard (Phase M2)

## Figure-by-figure narrative

### Figure 1 — Coupling network (`final_coupling_network.png`)
- Main result: Cross-domain coupling ranks are non-uniform; BAC↔ITS and EUK↔ITS occupy strongest tier.
- Biological interpretation: Shared or linked ecological gradients may synchronize these domains more strongly than AMF-centered pairings.

### Figure 2 — Environmental driver heatmap (`final_driver_heatmap.png`)
- Main result: Environmental explanatory profiles vary by pair/branch; pH-linked predictors recur among top contributors.
- Biological interpretation: Abiotic filtering is pair-dependent but converges on a core soil chemistry axis.

### Figure 3 — Plant hypothesis comparison (`final_plant_hypothesis_comparison.png`)
- Main result: Alpha-inclusive hypothesis performs best across multiple pair/branch combinations.
- Biological interpretation: Plant-diversity context is informative and non-redundant relative to abiotic structure.

### Figure 4 — Plant delta adjusted R² (`phase5c_model_delta_adj_r2.png`)
- Main result: Plant terms add modest but consistent explanatory increments over abiotic baselines.
- Biological interpretation: Plant effects are secondary-to-complementary rather than dominant.

### Figure 5 — Pair comparisons (`phase5c_pair_comparisons.png`)
- Main result: AMF-centered pairings show lower coupling magnitude but preserved plant-associated contribution.
- Biological interpretation: Lower coupling does not imply ecological irrelevance; mechanism may differ by domain role.

## Transition logic between results sections
1. Establish coupling hierarchy (Fig 1).
2. Explain coupling structure with abiotic layer (Fig 2).
3. Add plant-diversity explanatory layer (Fig 3-4).
4. Resolve domain-specific contrasts, especially AMF-centered patterns (Fig 5).

## Key biological interpretations
- Coupling strength and explanatory mechanisms are not interchangeable; strong coupling and strong plant association can dissociate by pair.
- pH remains a robust integrative axis across domains and model branches.
- AMF-centered configurations likely encode distinct ecological processes from BAC-inclusive couplings.

## Potential reviewer criticisms
1. Correlative synthesis may overstate mechanism without manipulative tests.
2. Network/coupling metrics may be sensitive to compositional and sparsity properties.
3. pH prominence may partly reflect broad covariation with other edaphic gradients.
4. AMF-centered interpretation may depend on marker resolution and taxonomic assignment constraints.
5. Cross-phase integration may combine metrics with different uncertainty structures; confidence communication should be explicit.
